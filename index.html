Discover Passive Cooling

Built for the Climate: Passive Cooling

Traditional UAE architecture was a direct response to the intense desert heat and humidity. Unlike modern buildings that rely on electricity, heritage homes and forts utilized **passive cooling** techniques—methods that cool a structure without active energy consumption.

Key features like thick walls, small windows, central courtyards, and the famous **Barajeel** (wind towers) worked together to create comfortable microclimates indoors. This design philosophy emphasizes sustainability and local resourcefulness.

Architectural Goal

The primary function was to reduce solar heat gain and increase internal air movement, often dropping indoor temperatures by $10-15^\circ \text{C}$ compared to the scorching exterior.

The Foundation: Local Materials

Coral Stone

Harvested from the seabed, this porous material was the main building block in coastal towns like Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Its porous nature helped insulate the interiors.

Sarooj (Gypsum Plaster)

A strong, durable plaster made from local lime and gypsum mixed with water. It was used to coat the walls, protecting them from the elements and giving a smooth finish.

Palm Fronds (Barasti)

The versatile fronds of the date palm were used for roofing and temporary structures. They provided excellent shade while allowing cooling breezes to pass through.

The Barajeel: An Ancient Air Conditioner

Unlocking the Wind Tower's Secret

The Barajeel is an aerodynamic marvel. It is a tall, chimney-like structure with openings on four sides that directs air down into the home. Click below to reveal the two simultaneous principles of physics that make the Barajeel function.

  • **1. Capture/Draft Effect:** The tower captures high-altitude breezes and directs cool air down to the rooms below. Regardless of wind direction, at least one side catches the moving air.
  • **2. Evaporative Cooling:** The downward-moving air often passes over a moist floor or fountain in the courtyard. The evaporation of water naturally cools the air, creating a chiller effect before it circulates into the living spaces.

The Legacy: Sustainability and Modern Design

Restoring the Past

Major restoration efforts, particularly in areas like the **Al Fahidi Historical Neighborhood** in Dubai and various heritage forts across the Emirates, ensure these structures are preserved as living history museums and active cultural hubs.

Inspiration for the Future

Today's sustainable architects in the UAE draw heavily on the principles of the Barajeel, integrating natural ventilation and light wells into cutting-edge projects to reduce carbon footprint and energy consumption, marrying tradition with modernity.
